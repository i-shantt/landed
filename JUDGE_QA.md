# JUDGE_QA.md — 25 likely questions, two-sentence answers

1. **Isn't this just an automated callback?** A callback asks "did you request this?" and a fraudster who controls the inbox or a lookalike phone can say yes. Landed asks the contact of record for a number only someone who can see the new account's statement knows, and the database only releases on an exact, single-use, case-bound match.
2. **The attacker sees the penny too.** Yes, and it does them no good: the amount is accepted only through the phone number or portal login established at onboarding, never through email or any contact offered in the request. An attacker who controls the mule account and the inbox still cannot speak through the vendor's number of record.
3. **What if they changed the phone number earlier?** A change to the contact of record is a separate human-attested step that is logged, and a request that changes bank and phone together goes straight to a human with no probe. A contact superseded after the case opens invalidates any confirmation that used it.
4. **Isn't Trouvé the same thing?** Trouvé holds by policy and places an automated call with a yes/no transcript judge. Landed adds a number-answer challenge instead of yes/no, a release gate enforced in the database and bound to vendor and account version, a landing check after payment, the recovery package, and an eval that includes the legitimate and AI-off paths.
5. **How is this different from Trustpair or Eftsure?** They validate accounts against networks of previously verified vendors, which excludes the small vendors a mid-market company actually pays. Landed is the control for the no-coverage case; a validation provider slots in first and the probe only runs on `no_coverage`.
6. **What does the probe prove, exactly?** Control, not legal ownership: that a contact already trusted for this vendor can state the amount credited to the new account. We say that wording on screen and in the docs, and never say "proves ownership."
7. **Why would vendors put up with this?** They already do: micro-deposits are how consumers link bank accounts, and vendors want to get paid to the right account. One call or portal visit per bank change, with the invoice released the same day, is less friction than a two-day hold with a phone tag.
8. **What about international vendors?** The probe needs a credit rail that reaches the account; for international vendors that is a wire or local rail with a longer settlement, and the clock model already handles arbitrary windows. The FBI Kill Chain eligibility in the package is computed from the payment's international flag.
9. **What if Nessie is down?** Nothing in the demo depends on it: the default rail is the simulated ledger, and the engine decides settlement and availability from its own timers. Nessie is a switch in Settings for the Capital One judge.
10. **How does the agent know when to ask a human?** It does not decide; the engine does, deterministically: wrong or missing statement, bank and contact changed together, confirmation window expired, portal attempts exhausted, validation mismatch, landing deadline missed. Each is an event in the timeline with the reason.
11. **Where could Claude be wrong here?** It could miss an implicit change in a PDF, so the deterministic guard still blocks any unverified destination; it could draft prose with a wrong number, so every number is checked against the deterministic fields; it could over-flag a benign email, so a human can reject the change. It cannot verify anything because it has no release path.
12. **Who is the customer and why would they pay?** The two-person AP team at a 50-to-500-employee company whose vendors are not in any verification network. They pay because one redirected payment is a $42,000 loss with a 56% recovery rate at best, and the control is cheaper than the insurance deductible.
13. **What did you simulate?** The bank (a ledger, with Nessie optional), time (a demo clock that drives the same timers), vendor answers (the eval or a human typing what the vendor sees; the voice channel is written but not dialed), and email authentication results. The engine, invariants, extractors, portal, audit log and eval are real.
14. **How do you know it works?** 91 unit and invariant tests, a scripted UI smoke test, and 20 scenarios run with the extractor on, off, and through Claude: zero false releases, every fraud and misdirection case blocked, every legitimate case landed with no human step. On scripted cases only; we say so on the Eval page.
15. **What stops your AI from approving a payment?** Structurally, nothing it calls can reach verification, payment, exceptional release or contact edits; those are human mutations with explicit arguments. The About page lists what it can and cannot call, as enforced in the database.
16. **What happens with the AI switched off?** The same thing: a clerk who types the attacker's account creates an unverified version and a case, and Pay is refused by the server. That is demo B.
17. **What if the vendor never answers?** The confirmation window expires, the case goes to a human, the invoice stays held, and nothing is lost. Scenario `judge_04`.
18. **What if the vendor says the wrong amount?** The release is refused with `amount_mismatch` and the case goes to a human; the portal allows three attempts. The amount is never shown anywhere a guess could be checked against.
19. **Can an AP clerk just pay the old account?** Open invoices follow the change request and are held until the new account is verified or a human rejects the change, which restores the previous account. Intake can re-point an invoice but can never make an account payable.
20. **What is the recovery package?** Deterministic facts (trace, amounts, accounts, timeline, Kill Chain eligibility) plus a bank fraud-line script, an indemnification letter draft, an IC3 narrative, an evidence checklist and an insurer note. Claude drafts the prose when available and every number in it is checked against the facts; recovery is never promised.
21. **How does this get into production?** The rail becomes a payments-API provider with Nacha Micro-Entry formatting, auth results come from the mail gateway, the vendor master and the hold move into the ERP payment run, and a validation provider runs before the probe. Each is behind an interface that exists today.
22. **How is the audit trail protected?** Events are append-only with no update or delete function, probe amounts and account numbers are redacted before insert, and account numbers are HMAC-hashed and AES-encrypted at rest. The case page exports the trail as JSON or a printable view.
23. **What is the SLA and why 24 hours?** The time the contact of record has to state the amount after the probe is available; it is a timer on the application clock, not a wall-clock delay. Twenty-four hours is a demo default; production would use one business day.
24. **Why does the Nessie amount show as 37 instead of $0.37?** Nessie stores amounts as integers, so we send cents with the unit in the description. The comparison that matters happens against our own probe table, not Nessie.
25. **What would you do next?** Dial the voice channel with a real test number, push the hold into an ERP, add a validation provider ahead of the probe, and run the eval against a corpus of real redirected-payment emails from an AP team willing to share them.
